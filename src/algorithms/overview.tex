\subsection{Motivation}

We begin by reviewing our problem statement. We wish to determine if $\CGrid$ is universal. If this is true, then by Theorem~\ref{thm:cgraph-vertex-minor-cgrid}, the
family of all circle graph states is universal. Moreover, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-width-vertex-minor-cgrid}, the same would hold for any
graph-state family to which that vertex-minor reduction applies. On the other hand, assuming $\BQP \neq \BPP$, Theorem \ref{thm:cgrid-efficiently-simulable} tells us that such universality cannot be efficient. Furthermore, if the comparability grid is not universal, unbounded rank-width is a necessary (by Theorem \ref{thm:measure-for-universal}) but not sufficient condition for universality (strengthening a result from \cite{circle-graphs}).

We saw in Section \ref{sec:mbqc} why this problem may not be as simple as it first seems. Notably, we saw how $\CompleteGraphs$ and any family consisting only of tree graph states cannot be universal for quantum computation. Informally, this tells us that both too few and too many edges may be problematic for universality. On the other hand, $\Grid$ is universal. However, through forbidden minors we see that any $\Grid_{3 \times 4}$ or larger cannot be a vertex-minor of any $\CGrid$. Thus, a proof of the universality of $\CGrid$ cannot involve a reduction to the universality of $\Grid$.

In this section, we introduce algorithmic tools that give evidence for the universality of comparability grids. These algorithmic tools can be divided into three parts, each addressing the following questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Section \ref{sec:subgraph-vertex-minor-alg}: Subgraph Algorithms. Can we conduct a census of small-size vertex-minors of $\CGrid$? What patterns emerge? What kinds of patterns may be advantageous for MBQC?
    \item Section \ref{sec:circuit-extraction}: Circuit Extraction. Given a subgraph of the comparability grid, what kind of quantum computations can we conduct on them through MBQC? Can we characterize this computational power through a set of quantum gates?
    \item Section \ref{subsec:lie-algebra-computations}: Universality Computations. Given a gate set, can we computationally determine whether it is universal? 
\end{enumerate}
