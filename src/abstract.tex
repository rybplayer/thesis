\section*{Abstract}

The measurement-based quantum computing (MBQC) model presents a way to do quantum computations by measuring a pre-prepared graph state. As such, there is interest in determining which graph states are universal for MBQC. The universality of the comparability grid is currently unknown and of some interest, since if it is universal, so are all families of graph states with unbounded rank-width. In this thesis, we present several approaches toward proving this universality, culminating in a construction conjectured to enable universal quantum computation on the comparability grid through MBQC.

\section*{Author's Note}

One of the great difficulties I faced writing this thesis was figuring out where to stop. In other words, what may I assume of the reader, and what must be explained? Past Mathematics undergraduate honors theses have been most helpful in this regard. Many of these take an introductory stance, writing for a mathematically mature but non-specialized audience (i.e. a senior mathematics undergraduate). 

For this reason, I have prepared a deliberately extensive exposition. For example, the ZX-Calculus, Clifford circuits, and several other concepts reviewed in this thesis are well-known to the quantum computing community. Nevertheless, many modern publications in this area review these concepts in their introductions \cite{circle-graphs,Backens_2021,McElvanney_2023}.

Finally, much of the research connected to this thesis remains ongoing. Rather than presenting a finished story, this thesis aims to record a path through the problem starting from first principles. I hope the reader may also find value and enjoyment in this path.
