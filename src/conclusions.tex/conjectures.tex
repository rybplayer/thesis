In Section 5, we introduced a series of algorithms for developing and testing finite graph gadgets. Given a tileable graph gadget $\Lambda$, we associated to it a parametrized gate $G_\Lambda$ and tested the approximate universality of the resulting gate family. In particular, the diagonal gadget fails the 3-qubit approximate universality test, while the width 3 gadget passes. 

In this section, we present a different kind of construction. Instead of searching for one finite tile, we define infinite families of gadgets $\hat\Lambda=(\Lambda_k)_{k\geq 1}$ where $\Lambda_k$ is intended to act on $k$ wires. 

\begin{definition}
\label{def:iterative-gadget}
An \term{iterative graph gadget} is a sequence
\begin{align}
    \hat\Lambda=(\Lambda_k)_{k\geq 1},
\end{align}
where each $\Lambda_k=(H_k,L_k,R_k)$ is a $k$-wire graph gadget. We write
\begin{align}
    L_k=(\ell^{(k)}_1,\ldots,\ell^{(k)}_k),
    \qquad
    R_k=(r^{(k)}_1,\ldots,r^{(k)}_k)
\end{align}
for its ordered left and right boundaries.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:middle-all-connected-gadget}
The \term{middle-all-connected iterative gadget}
\begin{align}
    \hat\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}
    =
    (\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_k)_{k\geq 1}
\end{align}
is defined inductively as follows. For \(k=1\), let
\(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_1\) be the one-edge line gadget. For \(k=2\), let
\(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_2\) be the $\Grid_{2 \times 2}$ gadget.

Suppose \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_k=(H_k,L_k,R_k)\) has been defined, with
$
    L_k=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k)
$, and $
    R_k=(r_1,\ldots,r_k)
$.
To construct \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_{k+1}\), take two copies
\(H_k^{\mathrm{left}}\) and \(H_k^{\mathrm{right}}\), and glue the right
boundary of the left copy to the left boundary of the right copy:
\begin{align}
    r^{\mathrm{left}}_j
    =
    \ell^{\mathrm{right}}_j,
    \quad
    \text{for every } j\in[k].
\end{align}
Let \(Q_k\) denote the result.

Now add three new vertices \(a_k,b_k,c_k\). The vertex \(a_k\) is adjacent to
\(b_k\) and to every left input of \(H_k^{\mathrm{left}}\). The vertex
\(c_k\) is adjacent to \(b_k\) and to every right output of
\(H_k^{\mathrm{right}}\). The vertex \(ck\) is adjacent to every vertex of
\(Q_k\), and also to \(a_k\) and \(c_k\). No other new edges are added.
Equivalently,
\begin{align}
    N(a_k)
    &=
    \{b_k\}\cup L_k^{\mathrm{left}},\\
    N(b_k)
    &=
    V(Q_k)\cup\{a_k,c_k\},\\
    N(c_k)
    &=
    \{b_k\}\cup R_k^{\mathrm{right}}.
\end{align}
The new boundaries are
\begin{align}
    L_{k+1}
    &:=
    (a_k,\ell^{\mathrm{left}}_1,\ldots,\ell^{\mathrm{left}}_k),\\
    R_{k+1}
    &:=
    (c_k,r^{\mathrm{right}}_1,\ldots,r^{\mathrm{right}}_k).
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:middle-all-connected-extension}
Let \(Q\) be a finite induced subgraph of some comparability grid, equipped with
a fixed coordinate embedding \(V(Q)\subseteq \mathbb Z^2\), and let
\(L=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k)\), \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_k)\) be ordered boundary sets.
Suppose \(Q\) is embedded so that there exist real numbers \(\gamma<\alpha\)
with
\[
\max_{p\in V(Q)\setminus L}y(p)<\alpha<\min_{j}y(\ell_j),
\qquad
\max_j y(r_j)<\gamma<\min_{p\in V(Q)\setminus R}y(p).
\]
Then one may add three vertices \(a,b,c\) so that the resulting graph remains
an induced subgraph of some \(\CGrid\), and
\begin{align}
    N(a)
    &=
    \{b\}\cup L,\\
    N(b)
    &=
    V(Q)\cup\{a,c\},\\
    N(c)
    &=
    \{b\}\cup R.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(P\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\) be the point set realizing \(Q\). Choose
\begin{align}
    x_-<\min_{p\in P}x(p),
    \qquad
    x_+>\max_{p\in P}x(p).
\end{align}
By the separation hypotheses, choose real numbers \(\alpha,\gamma\) such that
\begin{align}
    \max_{p\in V(Q)\setminus L} y(p)
    &<
    \alpha
    <
    \min_{j\in[k]} y(\ell_j),\\
    \max_{j\in[k]} y(r_j)
    &<
    \gamma
    <
    \min_{p\in V(Q)\setminus R} y(p),
\end{align}
and choose them with \(\alpha>\gamma\). Finally choose
\begin{align}
    \beta
    <
    \min\{\gamma,\min_{p\in P}y(p)\}.
\end{align}
Place
\begin{align}
    a&:=(x_-,\alpha),\\
    b&:=(x_- -1,\beta),\\
    c&:=(x_+,\gamma).
\end{align}

Since \(b\) is below-left of every point in \(P\), and also below-left of
\(a\) and \(c\), it is adjacent to every vertex of \(Q\) and to both \(a\)
and \(c\). Now \(a\) lies to the left of every point of \(P\). Hence \(a\) is adjacent to
a point \(p\in P\) exactly when \(\alpha\leq y(p)\). By construction, this
holds exactly for \(p\in L\). Thus
\begin{align}
    N(a)=\{b\}\cup L.
\end{align}
Similarly, \(c\) lies to the right of every point of \(P\). Hence \(c\) is
adjacent to \(p\in P\) exactly when \(y(p)\leq \gamma\). By construction, this
holds exactly for \(p\in R\). Thus
\begin{align}
    N(c)=\{b\}\cup R.
\end{align}
Finally, since \(x(a)<x(c)\) but \(y(a)>y(c)\), the vertices \(a\) and \(c\)
are incomparable. Therefore no other new edges are introduced, so the
extension is induced in \(\CGrid\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:middle-all-connected-cgrid}
For every \(k\ge 1\), the middle-all-connected gadget
\(\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}=(H_k,L_k,R_k)\) admits an induced embedding into a
comparability grid in which \(L_k\) is separated above the rest of the graph and
\(R_k\) is separated below the rest of the graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove by induction on \(k\).
The cases \(k=1\) and \(k=2\) are realized directly by the line gadget and the
\(2\times2\) grid gadget.

Assume \(\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}\) has such a separated embedding. Take two
copies, translate them far apart if necessary, and glue the right boundary of
the left copy to the left boundary of the right copy as in the definition. The
resulting graph \(Q_k\) again satisfies the separation hypotheses. Lemma~\ref{lem:middle-all-connected-extension}
then adds \(a_k,b_k,c_k\) without adding additional edges and produces the required
separated embedding for \(\Lambda_{k+1}^{\mathrm{mid}}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}
$\hat\Lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}$ is approximately universal for MBQC. That is, for every $k \in \N$, $\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}$ is approximately $k$-qubit universal.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Running the algorithm in Section \ref{subsec:lie-algebra-computations} on $\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}$ for $k \in [5]$ yields that $\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}$ is approximately universal for $k$ qubits for every $k \in [5]$.
\end{proof}

We now take a moment to discuss this result. First, note that the number of vertices in $\Lambda_k^{\mathrm{mid}}$ grows exponentially in $k$. This means that Theorem \ref{thm:cgrid-efficiently-simulable} does not eliminate the possibility that the conjecture is true. On the other hand, a proof for all $k \in \N$ is tricky primarily for two reasons. First, when constructing $\Lambda_{k+1}^{\mathrm{mid}}$, we introduce many new edges from $b$ to every other vertex. This almost certainly affects the computation of $H_k^\mathrm{left}$ and $H_k^\mathrm{right}$. Second, assuming we have shown that $\Lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{mid}}$ is approximately universal, this almost certainly does not imply that a single $G_{\Lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{mid}}}$ is $k$-qubit approximate universal. Indeed, we may need to apply $G_{\Lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{mid}}}$  many times to attain this. Both of these complications make a proof of the conjecture, and similar ones to it, challenging.

We now conclude this section and the thesis by presenting some future directions.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item An immediate next step is to develop an algorithmic pipeline that incorporates local complementation into every part of the construction. The hope is that local complementation may introduce the additional structure and tools needed to prove conjectures like the one above.
    \item Another future direction is to better characterize what makes families of graph states universal. In our particular case, a potential next step is to understand more precisely why the middle-all-connected construction works up to approximate $5$-qubit universality, and why other constructions don't.
    \item We may attempt a new approach to the problem. Suppose instead that we have a random variable that gives us induced subgraphs of $\CGrid$ that themselves contain some $\Grid$ as a subgraph. Can probabilistic methods be used to show universality this way?
    \item We have seen how the theory of vertex-minors and the rank-width measure are very powerful concepts for characterizing the universality of graph state families in general. What other measures are available, and can they be applied to the comparability grid?
\end{enumerate}
